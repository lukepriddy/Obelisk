Location-based immersive experiences

Geolocated audio, and everything after it.

Soundwalks. Audio tours. Locative storytelling. Obelisk does all of that, then keeps going: AI voices, characters people can talk to, AR objects, locked zones, and pages you can make look like your own. It runs in a browser, so nobody installs an app.

The short version

Most tools stop at sound.

Draw a geofence, drop in a track, and the audio plays when someone walks there. That part is solved, and Obelisk does it well.

But a place can hold more than a recording. It can hold someone to ask, a door that will not open until you work something out, an object that is only there through the camera. That is the part we built.

Zones

Every zone does what you tell it.

Set a radius as tight as a few feet or as wide as a field. Decide what happens when someone leaves, and whether the sound loops, plays once, or is gone for good.

Any radius Loop or play once Hidden from the map Fade in and out Quieter at the edges Open only after another zone

Voices

Write it. Hear it.

Type the words and pick a voice. ElevenLabs and Google voices are built in, so you get a narrator without booking a studio or recording yourself twelve times.

Characters

People they can actually talk to.

Give a character a voice, something they want, and something they are hiding. Players type to them and get real answers back. You decide what it takes before the secret comes out.

Real conversation A secret worth earning Stays in character Can open a locked zone

Puzzles

Doors that stay shut.

Lock a zone behind a password and hide the answer earlier in the walk. Leave a hint if you are feeling kind. Or let a character decide when someone has earned it.

Password zones Hints you write Unlocked by conversation Answers never leave the server

Collecting

Things to find and carry.

Hide objects along the route. Players pick them up, watch them gather in a counter as they walk, and spend them at the end. Some stay a mystery until found.

Hidden collectibles A counter on screen Spend them to get through Unknown until picked up

Camera

Objects that are only there through the lens.

Place something at a spot and it appears when a player raises their camera. It can sit still or fly overhead. All of it in the browser, with no app store in the way.

Augmented reality Sits still or flies over Placed on the map No install

Built for the real world

It waits until it knows where you are.

GPS guesses your location, and the guess gets better over the first few seconds. Most tools show a map right away and let the dot jump around. Obelisk holds the walk until the signal settles, so the first zone triggers where it should.

This is the real screen players see while their phone finds them.
