A character should make you earn the answer

You can put a character in front of a locked door. Talk your way past them and the door opens. It is a good idea and it is very old.

Ours did not work. The door opened as soon as the character replied to anything. A player typed hello, got a line back, and walked through.

Why that is worse than no puzzle

A door that opens for anyone is not a door. It is a short pause with extra steps. Worse, the player can tell. They know they did not do anything, so the moment lands flat.

Counting messages does not fix it

The obvious repair is to wait for a few messages before opening. We tried that idea on paper and threw it out.

If the door opens after four replies, the puzzle is not the puzzle. The puzzle is typing four times.

Let the character decide

Now you write what the character is hiding and what someone has to do to get it. Then the character judges it for itself, and tells the app quietly when it is satisfied. The player never sees any of that machinery. They just get an answer, and the door is open.

The trick is writing a condition the character can actually judge. Something like this works:

Reveals it only after the player writes you a poem of three lines. Any honest attempt counts. Asking you to write it does not.

Something like this does not:

Reveals it when the player is nice.

The first one has an answer. The second one is a mood, and a character asked to judge a mood will either open for everyone or for nobody.

Nobody gets stuck

The zone still has its normal password. Talking your way in is a shortcut, not the only door. If a player never works out what the character wants, they can still get through the ordinary way.
